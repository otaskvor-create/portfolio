Proom — license management web app

Proom is a closed, internal web application built to manage our customers' software licenses across every product. Longterm goal was a fully automated system that would save the company real work and long-term cost.

Problem

Until then, all license management and payments were handled in person—an outdated, inefficient process with a high risk of errors. The main challenge wasn’t designing the interface, but defining how the app should work and which features it actually needed to meet customer needs without becoming overly complex.

Research & process

Before a single sketch was drawn, we had to agree on what to expect from the application, so as not to design something faulty. It started with a detailed group discussion where me and our managers agreed on what the app should contain.

User flow

Feasibility Check

Every screen was discussed — around if it make sense and how difficult it would actually be to implement.

Sketches & wireframes

Low-fidelity wireframes in Figma, built in several versions before I settled on the one that felt right.

Team review

Final wireframes were reviewed with the whole team to confirm everything was designed correctly before I moved on.

UI design

A simple but modern visual identity, mostly white with a secondary blue, checking the competition throughout to avoid costly mistakes.

Wireframe vs final design

Solution

After handing the final prototype over to development, it was tested and confirmed ready to be programmed.

  • An overview dashboard with key stats — total users, licences and databases
  • A licences module tracking packages, users and expiration dates, split by investor/designer or contractor role
  • A billing section for contact and payment details, plus a full payment history log
  • A company-selection screen so one account can switch between the organisations it manages
  • Usability and accessibility rules followed throughout, so the final prototype was maximally usable
Color palette

Outcome

The next step was prototyping the individual interactions to simulate the finished product for final presentation. Once that was done, the project was handed to the development department, who gradually began programming it. This was the first version of an entirely new product, which continued to evolve and expand under other designers in the following years.
