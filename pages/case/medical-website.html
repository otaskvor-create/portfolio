Medical website redesign

Medical company's website had fallen behind — the content was solid, but its visual design, hierarchy and usability no longer met current standards. I redesigned it as a pilot: proof of a new visual direction and a foundation for a design system that could later scale to the rest of the site.

Problem

The core problem was an outdated visual treatment and an unclear content structure. Sections had no obvious hierarchy between them, the layout didn't support natural reading, and some elements felt visually inconsistent with one another.

Although the content itself was relevant, its presentation reduced readability and trust. For a medical product, the interface needed to feel professional, calm, and make information easy to find.

A further challenge was designing a visual language that wouldn't just work on one page, but stay flexible enough for future expansion across dozens of other content pages on the same site.

Design process

Analysis & wireframing

The project began with a detailed analysis of the original page — content structure, section order, visual hierarchy, text readability and how the page actually guided a reader through its information. This surfaced what was worth keeping and what needed to be reorganised or reworked visually.

I then prepared a simple wireframes to validate page layout before starting detailed visual design.

Digital sketch on left, final UI on right

UI design

The main part of the project was turning the wireframes into a detailed, high-fidelity design. Since the new visual language was meant to eventually influence the whole site, I iterated through several variants and paid close attention to consistency across every decision.

One of the first steps was reworking the colour palette. The original, strongly saturated turquoise felt dated and competed with the content itself, so I replaced it with a more muted blue that better supports trust, calm and a professional feel — typical of a medical environment. Typography was unified around Inter for its neutral character and strong readability across long-form text, headings and smaller informational elements.

New colour palette — muted blue replacing the original turquoise

The design leans on repeatable principles rather than one-off layouts: consistent text/visual placement, modular content blocks, reusable information cards, a clear typographic hierarchy, and unified spacing and radii — all so the same visual system could later be reapplied across other pages.

Solution

The result is a complete redesign of the pilot page, built around a new information hierarchy, a more modern layout and a unified visual language. The original expert content was kept intact but reorganised into clearer sections that support easier orientation and a natural reading flow.

  • A refreshed colour palette — a muted, trustworthy blue in place of the original bright turquoise
  • A unified typographic system built on Inter, with clear levels between headings, body text and supporting information
  • Modular, reusable content blocks and information cards, not dependent on a single photo or layout
  • Consistent spacing, corner radius and decorative elements across the whole page

Landing page

Outcome

The redesign introduced a direction that is noticeably more modern, professional and trustworthy than the original — improving orientation, separating topics more clearly, and helping readers spot the most important information faster.
